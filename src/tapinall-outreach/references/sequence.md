# Channels and sequence

The outcome is a sequence whose every step has a job, written to what large send datasets show, in the owner's voice. All figures below are vendor data from the vendors' own platforms or clients, not independent research; they agree on direction more than on numbers, so treat them as ranges.

## Choosing the channel

- Per prospect reached, Belkins' 2025 client data shows connect rates of 5.04% on LinkedIn against 0.45% on email, yet email still produced half of all booked meetings, because it scales. LinkedIn converts better per touch and is capped per week; email reaches more people per hour.
- Freelancers and founders with lists under about 50: both channels, sent by hand, deep tier. Hunter's 2025 data shows campaigns of 50 or fewer recipients averaging 5.8% replies against 2.1% for 1,000+, so a small, well-researched list is the stronger play.
- Agencies and anyone sending hundreds: email as the main channel, LinkedIn added for the deep tier.
- Creators: the platform where they post most, or the business email in their bio. Their recent post is the signal.
- Aim at the person who owns the problem. Gong found C-level executives 30.2% less likely to reply to cold email than other roles.

## Email: what the data says

- **Length.** Gong (2026) found the best reply rates at 50–100 words and a sharp drop above 100; Instantly's top senders average under 80 words. Hunter's 34M emails show only small differences between length bands (3.4–4.5%), so length is a ceiling, not the lever; relevance is. Step 1 runs 50–90 words; follow-ups are shorter.
- **Subject.** Belkins (5.5M emails, 2024) found 2–4 word subjects opened best, and personalised subjects got 7% replies against 3%. Gong also favours 1–4 words. Judge on replies, not opens: Apple Mail Privacy Protection inflates open rates. Write a plain 2–4 word subject from the signal or the problem; a fake "Re:" or "Fwd:" is deceptive under CAN-SPAM and burns trust.
- **Call to action.** In Gong's study of 304,174 emails, asking for interest ("Worth a look?") booked more meetings from cold emails than asking for a specific time; a specific time won only on active deals. Gong's 2026 guidance adds that an offer of value (a teardown, a benchmark, a short audit) beats a meeting request. Step 1 asks one low-effort question or offers one useful thing; ask for a time only after they reply.
- **Follow-ups.** Instantly (2025 data) finds 58% of replies come from step 1 and 42% from follow-ups, recommends 4–7 touches 3–4 days apart, and finds reply-style follow-ups (sent in the same thread, short, no new subject) do about 30% better than formal ones. Belkins (7.5M emails, 2025) finds steps 2–6 bring 58.6% of replies, step 3 alone 35.6% of booked meetings, and little gained after step 5; it recommends 3–5 steps. Default to 4 email steps on days 0, 3, 7 and 11; offer a 5th only for the deep tier.
- **Benchmarks.** Instantly's platform average reply rate is 3.43%; the top quarter reach 5.5% and the top tenth 10.7%. Hunter's 2025 average is 4.1%. Put these in the `.md` so the user judges results against them, and suggest pausing a campaign still under 1% after about 200 sends to fix the list or the angle (a rule of thumb, not a finding).

## Step jobs

Each step adds something new, because a follow-up that only "bumps" gives the reader no new reason to answer.

1. **Day 0.** The signal line; the problem it suggests, in the segment's words; one proof point from the profile's proof bank; the interest question or offer. No links beyond the signature, since links and images add spam risk for no gain on a first touch.
2. **Day 3.** In-thread reply. One new thing: a second proof point, a number, a short example relevant to the signal.
3. **Day 7.** A different angle or the offer of value: the resource, the audit, the benchmark. This is the step Belkins finds books the most meetings, so it carries the strongest offer.
4. **Day 11.** Short close: name the one thing you would send if they want it, and say this is the last note. No guilt and no fake deadline.

## LinkedIn

- **Note or no note.** In Belkins' 112,005 LinkedIn sequences, requests with a note were accepted slightly less often (25.3% against 27.6%) but those who accepted replied more (8.2% against 5.3%). LinkedIn's help centre says free and Basic members can add a note to only five requests a month; Premium is unlimited. So on a free account, spend the five notes on the deep tier, send the rest without a note, and carry the personalisation in the first message after acceptance.
- **Limits.** LinkedIn publishes no weekly number; vendors consistently report about 100 invitations per rolling week, and hitting the cap blocks invites for about a week. Plan the deep tier to fit, for example 20 a day on weekdays.
- **Length.** Notes are capped at 200 characters on free accounts and 300 on Premium (vendor-reported; the editor shows the live count). Write `linkedin_note` under 200 characters: the signal and a reason to connect, no pitch.
- **First message** (`linkedin_dm`). Send after acceptance: two or three sentences, the signal or a new observation, one question. Pitch-on-accept reads as a bait-and-switch.
- **By hand.** LinkedIn's User Agreement forbids bots and automation tools, and restrictions fall on the account, so the plan assumes manual sending.

## Voice and checks

Match the voice lines in the profile: a founder writes as one person, an agency can write as a team. Write at a plain reading level, short sentences, no jargon. Every proof point comes from the proof bank with its source; with an empty proof bank, the angle rests on the signal and the offer of value, and the `.md` says proof is missing. Run the anti-slop tests on the three sample leads and on the light-tier pattern before writing the rest.
