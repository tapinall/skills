# Rules the user must follow

The outcome is a short rules section in `tap-suites/creators/<slug>.md`, fitted to the platforms and countries in this run, that the user can paste into a creator brief or contract. Say in that section that it is general information, not legal advice, and that other countries have their own rules. Write it because in both the US and the UK the brand carries liability for what the creators it pays post.

## United States: FTC

The Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255, revised June 2023) and the FTC's "what people are asking" FAQ:

- A material connection must be disclosed: payment, free product, commission, employment, family. Free product counts even when unsolicited; ask creators who were gifted to disclose it and tag the brand.
- Clear and conspicuous means difficult to miss and easily understandable. In video, disclose at the start and preferably in both audio and on screen; in captions, before the "more" cut. Disclosures only in comments, a bio or a "disclosure" link are not enough.
- Wording that works: "Ad", "#ad", "Paid ad", "Sponsored by [brand]", "Gifted by [brand]", "Paid link" next to an affiliate link. Unclear: "#ambassador", "#partner", "#client", "thanks [brand]", "affiliate link", a bare "Sponsored" or "Gifted".
- Platform labels are not automatically sufficient; the FAQ recommends the creator's own disclosure as well.
- The advertiser must train and monitor its creators, periodically check what they post, and act on non-compliance; hiring an agency does not move that duty. Pre-approval is the practical route, especially for Stories that disappear.
- Audiences of children need more care; disclosures that work for adults may not work for them.
- The creator must actually have used the product, and cannot make claims the brand itself could not substantiate.

The Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (16 CFR Part 465, in force 21 October 2024) bans buying or selling fake indicators of social media influence (bot or bought followers, views, likes) for commercial purposes, and fake or undisclosed insider reviews. The FTC said it does not aim to hold a brand liable for unknowingly hiring a creator with fake followers, which is one more reason the vetting record is worth keeping.

## United Kingdom: ASA, CAP and CMA

The joint influencer guide, in its edition of October 2026 now resting on the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 and the CAP Code:

- Label ads upfront, visible before any click: "#ad", "#advert", "#advertising", "#advertisement", "#advertisementfeature". "If in doubt, label it."
- "Sponsored", "spon" and "sp" are now named as insufficient; the ASA has also ruled "gifted" and "PR trip" alone do not convey commercial intent. Platform features such as co-authoring are not always enough on their own.
- Payment includes gifts, trips and commission, not only fees.
- Paid reviews must reflect real use: no claiming to have tried what was not tried, no glossing over negatives.
- Keep disclosing in relevant content for 12 months after a commercial relationship ends.
- Everyone in the chain is responsible, brands included, and the CMA expects documented compliance processes. Under the DMCC Act the CMA can fine directly, up to 10% of global turnover; the ASA names non-compliant influencers and brands publicly and can run targeted ads to their followers.

## Platform tools

Use them in addition to the written label above, not instead of it.

- Instagram and Facebook: the creator tags the brand with the "Paid partnership" label; the brand approves the creator in its branded content settings. Partnership ads (running the creator's post as an ad) go through the same link.
- TikTok: the creator turns on the commercial content disclosure setting for any post promoting a brand, product or service, which adds a "Paid partnership" or "Commercial content" label. TikTok flags suspected undisclosed posts and gives 24 hours before the video loses For You eligibility, so undisclosed deals also cost the user reach.
- YouTube: the creator ticks "includes paid promotion", which shows viewers a disclosure at the start of the video.

## What goes in the rules section

For this run's countries and platforms: the label wording and placement, the platform toggle, pre-approval and monitoring (who checks, how often), what happens on a missed disclosure, the 12-month UK tail where it applies, and a note to agree usage rights and exclusivity in writing, since both change the price.
