# Whitespace and implications

The outcome is the `## Summary`, `## Whitespace` and `## Implications` sections: openings the evidence supports, and what each downstream skill should do with them. Those skills act on this section without reading the rest closely, so every line must point back to a row above.

## Finding whitespace

A gap is real where three things meet: buyers want it (voice-of-customer frequency), competitors do not serve or say it (positioning, pricing, ads), and the owner can credibly deliver it (profile Positioning and Proof bank). Two of the three is an observation; record it but give it low confidence. Kinds to test the evidence for:

- Unaddressed pain: frequent in low-star reviews across several competitors, absent from every headline and ad.
- Contradicted claim: a competitor promise its reviews dispute.
- Unserved segment: reviews from one kind of customer saying the product is not built for them.
- Pricing gap: an empty price band, or a pricing unit customers resent (per-seat complaints from growing teams).
- Message gap: claims every competitor shares, next to a customer outcome nobody names in the customers' own words.
- Channel gap: a platform where the audience is and no competitor advertises or publishes.
- Switching window: a competitor price rise, acquisition, outage or support cut with reviews reacting to it.

For each gap write the statement, links to at least two evidence rows from different sections, confidence (high when several sources and competitors agree; medium with one strong source; low for a single data point), and fit with the profile. Where the evidence suggests a sharper position than the profile states, propose it as a hypothesis in Dunford's terms (alternatives, unique attributes, value, target customer, category) for the owner to accept or reject; never rewrite `profile.md` here.

Rank gaps by evidence strength times fit, and put the top three to five in the Summary.

## Implications, per downstream skill

Write each subsection as instructions the skill can act on, citing the rows behind them.

- `### leads`: ICP refinements the evidence supports; trigger signals to filter accounts on (hiring for a role, a recent round, use of a competitor's technology, a competitor's price rise); competitor customer sources (case-study logos, reverse tech lookup); segments to exclude.
- `### outreach`: pains to open with, in customers' verbatim phrases; switching triggers that time a message; anxieties and habits to pre-empt as objections; comparisons it may make, limited to what a cited source shows, because a claim about a named competitor must hold up if challenged.
- `### seo`: competitor domains for keyword-gap work (organic and paid overlap); keyword seeds in customer language; comparison and alternative pages worth targeting ("<competitor> alternative", "<competitor> vs"); paid keywords competitors hold for months.
- `### social-content`: competitor handles to study; angles and hooks from customer phrases; long-running ad messages worth testing organically; themes every competitor already saturates.
- `### creators`: niches and platforms where the audience gathers; creators who appear in competitor ads or partnerships; categories with no creator activity yet.

The step is done when every gap links to evidence in two sections, every implication names its evidence, and Not collected lists what would change the conclusions if gathered.
