# Writing that is not AI slop

Readers and platforms both punish generic machine writing. In Raptive's 2025 survey of 3,000 US adults, content readers suspected was AI-made lost nearly half its trust and 14% of purchase consideration beside it. Meta, YouTube and TikTok cut reach or monetisation for unoriginal, templated and mass-produced content. B2B buyers name what they rate highly instead: strong research and data, help understanding their own challenges, and concrete guidance (Edelman–LinkedIn, 2024). Only 15% call what they read very good, so specific, sourced work stands out.

## The tests every piece passes

A piece is marked ready only when it passes all four:

1. **Only us.** It carries at least one element only this brand, person or run could supply: a first-hand story, a real number, a named customer or example, an opinion the owner defends, or a finding from this run's research. It comes from the proof bank in `tap-suites/profile.md` or from a research file, and the piece's notes cite which. Statistics, quotes, testimonials, case studies and client names are never invented; a fabricated proof point costs more trust than none, and can be unlawful in advertising.
2. **Swap test.** Put a competitor's name in place of the brand's. If the piece still reads true, it is generic: rebuild it around the only-us element.
3. **The reader's words.** It names the reader's situation in the language they use, taken from comments, reviews or replies in the research.
4. **Usable today.** A reader could act on it without asking anything.

## Tells to find and rewrite

Wikipedia's WikiProject AI Cleanup tracks these signs of chatbot writing, which shift as models change. Search drafts for them and rewrite in plain words:

- Vocabulary by era: "delve", "tapestry", "testament", "intricacies", "meticulously", "vibrant" (2023–24); "align with", "fostering", "showcasing", "enhance" (2024–25); "highlighting" (2025–26); "leverage", "multifaceted", "quietly", "universally" (2026). Also "crucial", "pivotal", "robust", "landscape", "interplay", "deep dive", "boasts" for "has", and "Additionally" opening a sentence. In 14 million PubMed abstracts, "delves" became 28 times more frequent after ChatGPT (Kobak et al., *Science Advances*, 2025).
- Inflated significance: "stands as a testament", "plays a pivotal role", "underscores its importance", "setting the stage for", "indelible mark".
- Constructions: "not X, but Y", "it's not just X, it's Y", reflexive lists of three, trailing "-ing" clauses that pad a sentence ("highlighting…", "ensuring…", "fostering…"), "serves as" or "stands as" where "is" belongs, vague attribution ("experts say", "studies show" with no source), formulaic "despite these challenges" closers, summary lines that restate the paragraph.
- Formatting: heavy bold, emoji opening headings or bullets, title-case headings, bullets that each start with a bold label, a run of em dashes.
- Chatbot residue: "I hope this helps", "Certainly!", "let me know", bracketed placeholders like "[Founder Name]", dates like "2025-XX-XX".

These are signals, not proof, and clearing them does not make generic content good; the four tests do. Wikipedia lists perfect grammar and formal prose as unreliable signs, so leave correct writing correct.

## Voice

Match the voice samples in the profile, real lines the owner wrote, over any adjective like "friendly". Write first person only for experience the owner actually had.

## Images and video

- AI depictions of real people or real products presented as real read as misleading (Getty, 7,500 respondents). Use real photos for people and products; use generation for illustration and concepts.
- Label realistic AI media where the platform requires it: Meta's "AI info", TikTok's AI-generated content label, YouTube's altered or synthetic content disclosure.
- Each piece in a series adds new information or a new perspective. A template with the nouns swapped is what YouTube's inauthentic-content rule and Meta's originality rules demote.

## Record it

Each finished piece notes where its only-us element came from and "slop check: passed", with what was rewritten.
