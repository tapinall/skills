# Prioritising

The outcome is the `## Opportunities` table: clusters ranked by score, each score traceable to rows already collected.

## Score three axes 1–5 and rank by the product

Multiplying means a cluster weak on any axis sinks, which is what should happen: big demand nobody can win, or easy wins nobody buys from, waste the owner's months.

- Demand: summed cluster volume, trend direction from Trends, and click share. Lower it a point for informational clusters with an AI Overview, where top-result CTR has fallen by a third to more than half in the studies in `keywords-intent.md`, unless the aim is citation.
- Winnability: who holds the top 10 now. Signs it is winnable: low-authority domains, forum or Q&A threads, thin or outdated pages, pages whose format misses the intent, or the user already ranking 8–20. Signs it is not: the top 10 filled with household brands, government or marketplace pages, or a page type the user cannot build (a SERP of tools when the user can only write). Compare authority with one provider's scores, the user against the top 10's median.
- Business value: how close the query sits to what the user sells. Transactional and commercial queries for a core offer score high, informational queries that lead to an offer score in the middle, curiosity traffic low. Ask the owner what each offer is worth when the profile does not say; it is the axis only they can judge.

Break ties by cost to build: improving an existing URL before a new page, a new page before a new section. Quick wins are queries where the user ranks 8–20 on a relevant URL, since the page exists and Google already connects it to the query.

## Clusters over keywords

Score the cluster, not its keywords one by one, where fetched SERPs confirm it. A cluster of twenty related long-tail queries with a shared top 10 often beats one head term with five times the volume, because one page can rank across all of them and the competition is thinner.

## Honesty in the table

Every score cites its rows (SERP date, volume provider, competitor tool). A score with nothing behind it is marked "guess". Where Search Console data is missing, winnability for the user's own positions rests on third-party estimates; say so in the row.

## Actions

Each row ends with one action: `new page`, `improve <URL>` (with what the SERP says the page lacks), `consolidate <URLs>` when two of the user's pages split one cluster, or `local` for Business Profile and local-pack work.

Done when every row has three scores with cited evidence and one action, and the table is sorted by score.
