# Content briefs

The outcome is one `### Brief: <primary keyword>` per chosen opportunity, which a writer or the owner can build a page from without having seen the research, and which gives the page something the pages ranking now do not have.

## Read the pages that rank

Extract the top three to five organic results for the primary keyword with `tap.web.extract` (`url`; cheap, often free). Skip forums and video, whose structure does not transfer, but note them: a forum thread in the top five means searchers want lived experience. For each page record its format, main headings, the subtopics and entities it covers, data, tools or media it uses, its last-updated date, and who wrote it and on what experience.

From those, build:
- Coverage baseline: subtopics most ranking pages cover. Searchers expect these, so the brief includes them, briefly.
- Information gain: what the searcher needs that no ranking page, or only one, gives. Sources: People Also Ask questions left unanswered, the audience's questions in the market file, gaps the AI Overview leaves, outdated facts, missing numbers, missing comparisons. Where the AI Overview already answers the basics, the page earns its click with what the summary cannot hold.
- First-hand experience: what the owner has done, seen or measured that bears on the query: their cases, numbers, photos, mistakes, opinions they defend. Take it from the profile's proof bank or ask the owner; where they have none for this topic, say so in the brief rather than invent it.

## The bar is Google's own

Google's guidance on helpful, people-first content asks whether a page provides "original information, reporting, research, or analysis", goes "beyond the obvious", and gives "substantial value when compared to other pages in search results". A brief that reorders what the top five already say fails those questions by construction, which is why information gain and experience are required fields, not extras.

E-E-A-T in the quality rater guidelines adds Experience to expertise, authoritativeness and trust; Google calls trust the most important. Raters do not set rankings, but they describe what Google's systems aim to reward. The brief names the author and the experience they bring, because Google asks whether it is "self-evident to your visitors who authored your content". Since the January 2025 rater guidelines update, pages whose main content is nearly all AI-generated or paraphrased with little added value get the lowest rating; Google's guidance also asks for a disclosure of how AI was used where readers would expect one.

Google's guidance says it has no preferred word count. Set length by what the outline needs, not by the competitors' average.

AI Overviews and AI Mode have, in Google's words, "no additional requirements" or "special optimizations"; they fan a query out into related subtopic searches. A page that answers the cluster's subtopics in clearly headed sections is what serves both the click and the citation.

## Scale is the trap

Google's spam policy on scaled content abuse covers "many pages … generated for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings", including generating pages with AI "without adding value", stitching content from other pages, and many pages that are near-copies with keywords swapped. Brief one page per cluster. Propose a set of similar pages (one per city, one per product variant) only when each can carry its own substance: local proof, distinct offers, real differences.

## Brief fields

- Target: primary keyword, the cluster's other keywords, country.
- Intent and format: the dominant page type on the SERP and its date; the page type to build.
- SERP features to answer: featured snippet format, People Also Ask questions, AI Overview presence.
- Ranking now: URL, format and what each covers, one line each.
- Coverage baseline.
- Information gain: each item with where the gap was seen.
- First-hand experience to add: each item with its source in the profile or the owner's answer, or "ask owner: <question>".
- Outline: H1, title options, sections in order.
- Evidence to include: data, examples, media, each with its source.
- Internal links: pages on the user's site to link from and to.
- Author and disclosure: who writes it, the experience they bring, how AI was used.

Pass every brief through the four tests in `anti-slop.md`; the swap test catches a brief any competitor could have written.

Done when each chosen opportunity has a brief with every field filled or marked as a question for the owner, and every gap and experience item cites its source.
